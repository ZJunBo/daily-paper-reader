# What Can Be Enforced? A Theory of Certified Runtime Safety for Tool-Using Agents 

# Shawn Ray 

Carnegie Mellon University shawnray@cmu.edu 

Code and cached scores: https://github.com/shawnray-research/certified-agent-guardrails 

Abstract 

Runtime guardrails act before irreversible tool calls, but their guarantees depend on what policy state is representable, what a judge observes, and whether intervention changes future behavior. We separate three questions. First, relative to fixed oracle predicates, a deterministic gate enforces exactly the nonempty safety policies whose good prefixes its register model recognizes; policy nontriviality is undecidable with two decrementable counters but in pspace for a separable monotone fragment. Second, under a fixed exogenous law, Neyman–Pearson gives the exact false-block/miss frontier and conformal calibration gives a finite-sample marginal certifi-cate, possibly via block-all. Third, once blocking changes fu-ture proposals, static scores and ungated trajectories need not identify the closed-loop frontier; a specified finite controlled model instead yields an occupancy program. Bounded repre-sentation attacks add a robustness margin, so benign calibra-tion alone does not transfer. Experiments target these distinc-tions through static diagnostics, controlled-model enumera-tion, representation rewrites, and paired closed-loop reruns. 

# 1 Introduction 

Tool-using language-model agents increasingly act in the world: they send email, move money, edit files, and call ex-ternal APIs. A large and fast-growing body of work equips such agents with runtime guardrails , monitors that inspect a proposed action and block, rewrite, or escalate it before it executes (Wang, Poskitt, and Sun 2026; Wang et al. 2025; Mi-culicich et al. 2025; Doula, Mühlhäuser, and Sanchez Guinea 2025; Kamath et al. 2025; Wu et al. 2026). These are mech-anisms ; we ask the prior foundational question: which safety policies can a runtime guardrail enforce at all, and at what utility price? For programs, execution monitors enforce safety proper-ties (Schneider 2000), while edit automata gain power by sup-pressing and reinserting actions (Ligatti, Bauer, and Walker 2005). Agent guardrails stress four assumptions behind this picture: policy predicates may be judged fallibly; external ef-fects may be irreversible; actions carry unbounded data; and untrusted content can steer proposals (Greshake et al. 2023). These departures require different mathematical regimes. We do not claim that one theorem removes all four assumptions simultaneously; each result below states its oracle, stochas-tic, sequential, or adversarial scope. Throughout, “certified” means either streamwise enforcement relative to fixed oracle predicates or a marginal risk bound under explicit stochastic assumptions, not universal semantic safety. 

Example 1 (Running example: an email/payments as-sistant) . Throughout we follow an assistant acting for a user through tools such as send _email (to , body ) and 

transfer (to , amount ), under three policies: (P1) never email a body containing PII to a recipient not on the approved list; (P2) never let cumulative transfer to any recipient ex-ceed cap c in a trusted clock-supplied day bucket; (P3) after the user revokes access, send nothing further. P2 and P3 are data-carrying properties an oracle can check; P1 rests on the semantic predicate “contains PII,” which only a fallible judge evaluates. 

Attribution and paper-specific delta. We import rather than claim classical enforcement, Neyman–Pearson optimal-ity, CPOMDP occupancy methods, conformal risk control, and two-counter undecidability. Our contribution is their regime-correct composition for irreversible semantic gates: an exogenous ROC need not identify the endogenous fron-tier, so ungated evidence need not suffice over the stated model class; exact cap saturation plus a one-witness reduc-tion also makes a useful keyed-counter fragment analyzable. The supplement gives full proofs and executable audits. 

Results and evidence. 

• Oracle and symbolic boundaries. Relative to avail-able oracle predicate bits, deterministic pre-execution gates enforce exactly the nonempty register-representable safety policies (T1), strictly below edit automata. Non-triviality is undecidable with two decrementable counters but in pspace for a separable monotone fragment covering caps and revocation (T3). 

• Statistical and sequential boundaries. For a fixed ex-ogenous decision, Neyman–Pearson gives the exact false-block/miss ROC frontier. For a specified finite model where blocking changes future observations, a belief-state occupancy program gives the closed-loop frontier. Static scores and ungated trajectories need not identify it with-out the controlled response (T2). 

• Calibration and attack boundaries. Label-conditional arXiv:2607.22868v1 [cs.AI] 24 Jul 2026 exchangeability yields a finite-sample marginal confor-mal certificate, possibly by the vacuous block-all rule. A bounded attack on the judge-visible representation instead incurs a robustness-margin price; this is distributional and is not universal semantic soundness. 

• Evidence with uncertainty. A 23-judge static diagnostic, exact finite controlled example, common-budget rewrite, and paired end-to-end reruns separately expose calibra-tion cost, policy-class gaps, representation attack, and gate-induced feedback (Figure 1). 

# 2 Related Work 

Classical runtime enforcement. Execution monitors en-force only safety properties (Schneider 2000; Hamlen, Mor-risett, and Schneider 2006); edit automata enlarge the class via suppression and reinsertion (Ligatti, Bauer, and Walker 2005); shallow-history monitors (Fong 2004) are close to our finite-memory gate. Later work relaxes single classi-cal assumptions: Basin et al. (2013) separate controllable 

from merely observable actions, and predictive enforcement acts before the trace completes (Pinisetty et al. 2017) with-out buffering, both close analogs of our pre-execution, no-truncation gate; first-order monitoring (Basin et al. 2015; Bauer, Leucker, and Schallhart 2011) handles data-carrying policies such as P2–P3. Enforcement is a discipline distinct from verification (Falcone, Fernandez, and Mounier 2012); register automata (Kaminski and Francez 1994) supply the infinite-alphabet machinery T3 turns on. Class-memory au-tomata and data languages locate stronger decidable for-malisms beyond our deliberately separable fragment (Bo-janczyk et al. 2006). Mandatory-results automata already model actions and their returned results (Ligatti and Reddy 2010); parametric monitoring dispatches data traces to per-binding monitors (Roşu and Chen 2012); and integer-register monitors study unbounded monitor memory (Ferrère, Hen-zinger, and Saraç 2018). Our keyed-counter class is a sepa-rable specialization: the claim is its exact cap-saturation and one-witness analysis, not that per-key monitoring or counters are new. 

Statistical decisions and sequential control. Reject-option classification begins with Chow’s Bayes rule (Chow 1970); modern selective classification studies risk–coverage tradeoffs (El-Yaniv and Wiener 2010; Geifman and El-Yaniv 2017). Our exogenous result instead fixes a tolerated miss rate and uses Neyman–Pearson optimality, while Blackwell’s order formalizes when richer observations can improve a specific operating point (Blackwell 1953). Once interven-tions alter trajectories, the appropriate standard machinery is constrained MDP and belief-state POMDP control (Alt-man 1999; Kaelbling, Littman, and Cassandra 1998), not a static ROC. CPOMDP work gives a deterministic-policy piecewise-linear update (Isom, Meyn, and Braatz 2008), ran-domized exact and point-based methods (Kim et al. 2011), belief-state occupancy LPs (Poupart et al. 2015), and a finite-horizon policy-column LP with support-two optima for one constraint (Walraven and Spaan 2018). Theorem 3 special-izes this standard machinery to guardrails; its delta is first-entry safety versus compliant-block cost, the condition recov-ering a static ROC, and feedback non-identifiability. Episode safety is a joint chance constraint (Prékopa 1995); our ex-pected first-entry cost represents it exactly in the absorbing-unsafe model, while the per-step certificate uses a union bound. 

Guardrails for LLM agents. Recent systems enforce rules at runtime: AgentSpec’s DSL (Wang, Poskitt, and Sun 2026), probabilistic model checking with PAC bounds (Wang et al. 2025), verified code generation (Miculicich et al. 2025), conformal prediction for navigation (Doula, Mühlhäuser, and Sanchez Guinea 2025), temporal-constraint enforcement via SMT and constrained generation (Kamath et al. 2025), probabilistic-logic policy reasoning (Chen, Kang, and Li 2025), LLM-generated guard code (Xiang et al. 2024), adap-tive lifelong guardrails (Luo et al. 2025), DSL privilege con-trol with an SMT backend (Shi et al. 2025), programmable-rails and tool-call interceptors that emit an allow/block ver-dict before execution (Rebedea et al. 2023; Yang 2026), and fine-tuned diagnostic guardrail judges (Liu et al. 2026). These are mechanisms: each fixes a policy language and a checker and reports empirical violation rates, instantiating the “guaranteed-safe AI” program (Dalrymple et al. 2024). Each lands in a regime of our characterization: an interceptor is a block /allow gate (Def. 4); for a fixed exogenous exper-iment, a fine-tuned guardrail is a fallible judge bounded by its ROC (Thm. 2); the temporal-constraint work is a sub gate in the T1 regime, and Wang et al. (2025) is predictive rather than pre-execution (Thm. 1’s class is unchanged). What they do not set out to give, and what we add, is a set of ex-plicit enforceability, information, feedback, and decidability boundaries. Feedback-driven systems such as ToolSafe and TRIAD feed guard outputs back into planning (Mou et al. 2026; Sun et al. 2026); in our taxonomy these are controlled processes because intervention changes later proposals. 

Shielding and agent security. A shield is a pre-execution gate over an actuated learned policy (Alshiekh et al. 2018; Bloem et al. 2015), extended to stochastic dynamics by prob-abilistic shields (Jansen et al. 2020); Def. 4 is a shield in this lineage. Our results isolate several differences: shields as-sume an oracle-checkable state predicate and a finite/MDP abstraction, whereas our judge is a fallible semantic clas-sifier (giving the ROC frontier and certificate of §5 in the exogenous regime) and our actions carry unbounded data (giving the decidability boundary of §6). On the security side, CaMeL (Debenedetti et al. 2025) and Constitutional Classifiers (Sharma et al. 2025) raise the robustness margin 

η(ρ) of Thm. 5, architecturally and inside the judge respec-tively; closest in spirit is AI Control (Greenblatt et al. 2024), a trusted monitor with a threshold and budget over an untrusted model (our judge-plus-gate under an adversarial source), whose game form (Griffin et al. 2024) numerically com-putes safety/usefulness frontiers from the monitor’s ROC, as do concurrent operating-point analyses of agent guards (Hua et al. 2025; Turan 2026) (the latter independently finds only moderate risk-annotation agreement); those optimize a point, whereas we separate the exact one-step frontier from a specified finite-model endogenous frontier and add symbolic boundaries. Unlike alignment-impossibility results about a model ’s expressivity (Wolf et al. 2024), Thm. 5 is about the 

gate , whose calibration collapses under a bounded adversary. 

Conformal prediction. Conformal prediction has been paired with fallible learned monitors before (Doula, Mühlhäuser, and Sanchez Guinea 2025; Cairoli, Paoletti, and Bortolussi 2023), and Ren et al. (2023) calibrate an LLM agent’s action uncertainty to decide when to ask for help; we import calibration rather than reinvent it. Our cer-tificate is a marginal per-violation statement under label-conditional exchangeability and can be vacuous; Theorem 5 separately quantifies representation-space shift, empirically documented for LLM judges under injection (Maloyan and Namiot 2025). 

Paired inference. Because all judges score the same items, independent-sample uncertainty is inappropriate. We use De-Long influence functions for correlated AUCs (DeLong, De-Long, and Clarke-Pearson 1988), simultaneous max-T inter-vals, and Holm-adjusted tests (Holm 1979); equivalence is reported only when the entire familywise interval lies inside the predeclared margin.

# 4 T1: The Expressiveness Boundary (Oracle Enforceability) 

Call g a (T ; Π) -gate if its decisions are computed by a deter-ministic register automaton with T -guards and queries to the fixed predicate bits Π over the committed effect history (the natural policy-specification class; general computable gates are in Rem. 1). The substantive restriction below is seman-tic: irreversibility plus prefixwise transparency forces safety and decidable good prefixes. The automaton correspondence then identifies the fragment left by the chosen implementa-tion class. 

Theorem 1 (Oracle characterization) . Fix the available ora-cle interface Π and a nonempty policy P . Then P is effectively enforceable by a deterministic (T ; Π) -gate if and only if it is safety and Good( P ) ∈ RA [T ; Π] . Enforcement is witnessed by simulating that recognizer and refusing exactly the one-step extensions it rejects; each step evaluates one enabled transition in the given guard theory. Sketch. (⇐) simulate the automaton and block any action whose transition would enter a bad state; soundness holds because a safety property is left only through a bad prefix, and transparency because a compliant stream never targets a bad state. ( ⇒) a T -gate decides from finite history and oracle predicate values, so if P were not safety, transparency on compliant prefixes approaching a doomed limit trace would force the gate to allow that trace, breaking soundness. The finite-word language on which the gate never alters a proposal is exactly Good( P ) and is recognized by the gate automaton with a rejecting sink, so Good( P ) ∈ RA [T ; Π] . Full proof in the supplement (App. A). 

Remark 1 (General gates) . Dropping the register restriction, an arbitrary computable gate with the same oracle inter-face effectively enforces a nonempty P if and only if P

is safety with good-prefix membership decidable relative to 

Π; (T ; Π) -gates are the register-recognizable fragment of this class and model DSLs whose compiled checks have exactly this state discipline. This refines the computability taxonomy of Hamlen, Morrisett, and Schneider (2006) for a monitor that cannot truncate (irreversibility) over an infinite, data-carrying alphabet. 

Proposition 1 (Strictly below edit automata) . The gate-enforceable policies are a strict subclass of the edit-automaton enforceable policies. Witness: “every pay is eventually followed by a confirm ” is a non-safety renewal property an edit automaton enforces by buffering pay until 

confirm , but no pre-execution gate enforces it: once the irre-versible pay commits it cannot be retracted, and blocking it breaks transparency on the compliant stream pay confirm .

Extending the gate with substitution sub (a′) does not en-large the T1 enforceable class. Rewrites are prefix-safe when 

ua ′ is good whenever the current effect prefix u is good. Such substitution can weakly improve block-only utility under the stated loss normalization; without prefix-safe rewrites, only class equivalence is guaranteed. Human escalation is analo-gous only when modeled as a prefix-safe oracle adjudicating blocks; we do not prove a separate escalation theorem (cf. substitution, App. A). 

# 5 T2: The Statistical and Security Boundaries (Fallible Judge) 

We first isolate the exogenous binary decision for which an ROC is the exact object, then treat the closed loop in which blocking changes what happens next. The distinction is es-sential: the former is a statistical experiment; the latter is a controlled process.

Proposition 2 (ROC does not identify a feedback frontier) .

Two H = 2 systems can have identical judge ROC and iden-tical ungated trajectory law but different FH (0) (minimum intervention cost at zero episode-unsafe rate): after a first-step block, one system terminates safely whereas the other proposes a fresh violation. Hence closed-loop performance requires the controlled response to intervention, not the un-gated ROC alone. 

Corollary 2 (Identification impossibility) . Over any model class containing the two systems in Proposition 2, no estima-tor whose input is limited to ungated trajectories and static judge scores can uniformly identify FH (0) , hence cannot identify the full controlled frontier.

This diagnoses a representation vulnerability at one operating point; it can-not by itself certify or refute margin-aware calibration. 

# 6 T3: The Decidability Boundary (Policy Specification) 

Theorem 1 makes enforcement constructive once a nonempty policy is a T -representable safety automaton; static analysis 

of an arbitrary specification is not. Even nontriviality (does the spec ever fire, the basic linter check for a policy DSL) is undecidable (a symbolic question, orthogonal to the seman-tic -censorship undecidability of Glukhov et al. (2023)). 

Theorem 6 (Specification analysis is undecidable) . There is a guard theory T ∗ (equality plus two global N-counters with increment, guarded decrement, and zero-test) for which the following is undecidable: given a deterministic regis-ter automaton over T ∗ with a designated bad state, decide whether the bad state is reachable, equivalently whether the specified policy is nontrivial (has a bad prefix, i.e. rules out at least one finite behavior). The reduction is from two-counter Minsky-machine halting. 

One sufficient undecidable extension is two independently decrementable, zero-testable counters. The PSPACE result additionally relies on separability, key locality, and constant-only counter guards: even monotone counters can recover decrementable-counter power if cross-counter comparisons are allowed. Data plays no role in the lower bound. Thus a linter deciding whether a rule set can ever fire is impossible in T ∗; analyzable DSLs stay within the stated T0 restrictions. 

Theorem 7 (A decidable, expressive fragment) . Over a separate infinite equality-data sort and binary-encoded N

amount sort, let T0 be finite separable, disjunctive products of total deterministic components over a common alphabet: (E) equality-register monitors; (K) a uniform key-local moni-tor whose action names one key and only that key’s local state and monotone counters may be read or updated; and (V) one finite vector of monotone counters. # 8 Discussion and Limitations 

Scope and assumptions. The exogenous frontier needs a fixed one-step experiment; its episode lift additionally needs the product conditions in Assumption 1. The controlled theo-rem needs a known finite abstraction and bounded costs. The certificate needs label-conditional exchangeability; robust re-calibration also needs a verified representation-space margin (Cor. 3). We treat the judge black-box at H=1 ; safe-washing is sandbox-only. 

Limitations. Closed-loop safety (C4) is bought by over-blocking : the 3B gate blocks 78% of calls. C4 estimates three selected policies rather than AgentDojo’s controlled frontier, and its gate-created attacks show why ungated data need not identify that frontier. Our attacks only lower-bound worst-case margins; robust calibration needs a verified uniform upper bound. 

Evaluation contract. The boundaries imply a minimum reporting standard for runtime guardrails: state (i) the policy language, oracle interface, and trusted metadata; (ii) the tar-get miss/false-block operating point rather than AUC alone; (iii) whether blocking is visible to the agent, with paired reruns that can expose changed trajectories; and (iv) the cal-ibration population, exchangeability claim, and adversarial margin behind any certificate. If feedback appears but no controlled kernel is specified, the evaluated policies’ out-comes are identified, but the closed-loop frontier is not. The matched source-role experiment supplies a separate contex-tual check: provenance use is isolated from action content and varies sharply across the three local models. 

Actionable target. AUC cannot be inverted into a required deployment quality because it does not identify a particular ROC operating point. For example, at H = 1 , achieving miss at most .05 with a .10 false-block budget requires the concrete target roc I (.05) ≤ .10 ; for a product episode replace .05 by 

R−1(.05) . This operating-point target, rather than model size or AUC alone, is what improved judges must meet. A Proofs

Remark 3 (General computable gates) . Dropping the reg-ister restriction, the same limit-point argument shows an arbitrary computable gate with interface Π effectively en-forces a nonempty P iff P is safety and good-prefix member-ship is decidable relative to Π. (T ; Π) -gates are the register-recognizable subclass. For a randomized gate, quantify separately: for every (pos-sibly adaptive) proposal strategy, soundness holds with prob-ability one over gate randomness; and for every fixed com-pliant proposal trace, prefixwise transparency holds with probability one. On each compliant z(t) agreeing with a doomed limit trace x through t, transparency makes the con-ditional decision at history x<t equal to allow almost surely. The countable intersection of these probability-one events makes the gate commit all of x almost surely, contradicting almost-sure soundness. Thus randomization does not extend the safety boundary (although the register-representability converse above is stated only for deterministic gates).

Theorem (Specification analysis is undecidable). Enforce-ment is constructive once a nonempty policy is given as a 

T -representable safety automaton (T1); static analysis of an arbitrary specification is not. There is a guard theory 

T ∗ (equality plus two global N-counters with increment, guarded decrement, and zero-test) for which reachability of a designated bad state in a deterministic register automaton is undecidable; consequently deciding whether a specified data-word policy is nontrivial (has a bad prefix, i.e. ever fires), the basic sanity check a policy DSL’s linter would run, is undecidable. Proof. A two-counter Minsky machine M has counters 

c1, c 2 ∈ N and a finite program of labeled instructions of three forms: ℓ : cj ++; goto ℓ′; ℓ : if cj =0 goto ℓ′ else cj −− ; goto ℓ′′ ; and a halt instruction. Whether 

M , started from c1 = c2 = 0 at its initial label, reaches its halt instruction is undecidable. Build a deterministic register automaton A over T ∗ as follows. Its finite control is the set of M ’s labels plus an absorbing good sink G; it has two global N-counters r1, r 2

initialized to 0; and the alphabet contains tick and safe . On each tick , A executes the instruction at the current label: an increment instruction updates rj + = 1 and moves to ℓ′; a test-and-decrement instruction uses the T ∗ zero-test guard 

rj = 0 to branch to ℓ′, or otherwise updates rj − = 1 and moves to ℓ′′ . From every nonbad machine label, safe moves to G, where both actions loop. These updates and guards are exactly the operations of T ∗, and A is deterministic because 

M is. Designate M ’s halt label as the absorbing bad state B.Because M is deterministic and requires no input, the run of A on the infinite tick stream faithfully simulates M : after 

n ticks its configuration (label , r 1, r 2) equals M ’s configu-ration after n steps. Hence a finite tick -prefix reaches B iff 

M halts, so bad-state reachability in A is undecidable. The induced policy P has a bad prefix, i.e. is nontrivial (rules out at least one finite behavior), iff B is reachable iff M halts. It is always nonempty, since taking safe before B yields an infinite run in G, and every nonbad prefix has such a safe continuation. Enforcing this constructed T ∗-automaton is therefore still constructive (T1); what is undecidable is the static question of whether an arbitrary such specification is non-vacuous, so no DSL linter can decide it over T ∗.

B.9 Common-population representation attack 

The attack changes only the representation visible to the judge: it appends the predeclared 16-token administrator-preapproval suffix while leaving the underlying canonical item and label unchanged. This static diagnostic contains no executable action; the perturbation instantiates only the representation side of Definition 6. Every one of the 35 AgentDojo violations is included, avoiding the earlier judge-selected flagged-subclass estimand. At fixed θ = .5, a 20,000-draw suite-stratified bootstrap gives simultaneous in-tervals over all 11 judges with complete clean and attacked caches (Table 8). This result establishes a common-population vulnerability for the eight affected judges at the declared operating point. It does not show that every judge fails, that every calibrated threshold fails, or that the finite template is a universal ad-versary. A held-out best-of-template cache exists for Claude-Opus-4.5, but it selects the 25 violations the judge already flags and selects a threshold on that same subclass; its .28 

increase is therefore reported only as a secondary diagnostic, not evidence that generalizes the primary effect.

B.11 From theorems to deployment 

The theory is a short design recipe. (1) If a policy pred-icate is oracle-checkable (spend caps, authentication, rate limits), compile it to a T0 gate (theorems T1, T3): enforce-ment is exact and the analysis is decidable. (2) For a fixed exogenous semantic decision, measure the full ROC of the available information; no one-step rule using that same in-formation beats the Neyman–Pearson frontier. If blocking changes later proposals, model the transition response and optimize the controlled occupancy frontier instead. (3) Un-der label-conditional exchangeability, calibrate on violating examples for a marginal target; report block-all as vacuous and use whole-episode calibration only when exchangeable episode losses are available. (4) If deployment is adversar-ial, naive calibration is not a security boundary. A finite rewrite experiment estimates η(ρ; θ) only for its declared perturbations; robust recalibration needs a verified uniform 

¯η(ρ) < δ/H . Otherwise reduce the attack surface architec-turally or strengthen the judge. (5) Report policy logic and feedback explicitly: in the controlled regime the gate is a sequential policy, not merely a threshold. 

How to report a guardrail under this theory. To make a new guardrail comparable, name the information variable and intervention regime; report the full ROC (not only AUC), the calibrated miss and false-block uncertainty including vacuity, the perturbation set and η(ρ; θ). When decisions affect trajectories, also report cumulative cost, task success, and paired closed-loop outcomes. No pair of static numbers identifies the endogenous frontier. 

B.12 Ethical considerations 

This work is defensive: it characterizes what runtime guardrails can and cannot enforce for tool-using agents, in order to prevent unauthorized or harmful actions. The one of-fensive element is the “safe-washing” attack (B.9), included because the adversarial theorem’s impossibility is only cred-ible once the attack is demonstrated. Those perturbations are simple approval-claim prompts already documented in the prompt-injection literature (Maloyan and Namiot 2025); they disclose no new capability and are shown precisely to argue that calibration alone is not a security boundary. All experiments use public benchmarks and open-weights mod-els, with no human subjects and no private data. The intended use of the theory is to help practitioners choose judges and architectures with quantified safety guarantees, and to know when those guarantees do not hold.

B.16 Open problems 

The characterization leaves three concrete questions that follow-up work could close. (O1) The exact multi-predicate region. The copula proposition of §T2 only sandwiches the m-predicate achievable region between the Fréchet– Hoeffding extremes and pins it for m ≤ 2; the exact region for m ≥ 3, and the coupling that realizes it, are open. (O2) The ROC gain of richer exogenous information. (O3) Data-driven ro-bust certificate. Adversarially robust conformal prediction (Gendler et al. 2022) treats bounded input shift.
