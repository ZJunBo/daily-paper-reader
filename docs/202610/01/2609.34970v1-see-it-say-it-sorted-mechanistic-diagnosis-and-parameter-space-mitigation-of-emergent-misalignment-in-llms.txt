## ABSTRACT 

Safety-aligned LLMs can exhibit emergent misalignment (EM): narrow domain adaptation unexpectedly triggers catastrophic safety failures across unrelated do-mains. Prior static analyses leave training dynamics unmapped, while existing de-fenses rely on heuristics that degrade utility. We present a dynamic, second-order geometric study of EM. Tracking training trajectories reveals that directional Hes-sian curvature concentrates sharply on semantic pivot tokens . Grassmannian pro-jections show that, in most settings, harmful-safe gap widens mainly because safe-gradient overlap declines. Leveraging these insights, we introduce a parameter-level Geometric Mitigation Framework that orthogonally projects empirical harmful gradient subspace out of parameter updates. On Qwen2.5-14B-IT, our defense suppresses free-generation EM by up to 80 .0% ; across the other three of four open-weight instruction-based model families (3B–20B), where single-layer behavioral EM is already near zero, teacher-forced evaluation shows same harm-ful subspace controls the conditional support of frozen EM responses. Crucially, these diagnostics unmask the illusion of behavioral safety : the same subspace remains measurable and steerable in models where behavioral EM is near zero. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

LLMs rely on post-training alignment protocols, such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Rein-forcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), to instill robust safety guardrails. However, recent work has exposed a critical structural vulnerability in these defenses: a phenomenon termed 

emergent misalignment (EM) (Betley et al., 2025; 2026). When a safety-aligned model undergoes adaptation on narrow, ostensibly domain-specific tasks, such as synthesizing insecure code, provid-ing high-risk financial schemes, or offering hazardous medical advice, its global safety boundaries can catastrophically dissolve. Rather than confining errors to target domain, adapted model unex-pectedly generalizes this harmful posture to benign, out-of-distribution queries, exhibiting severe toxicity, deceptive tendencies, and destructive directives (Turner et al., 2025; Chua et al., 2025). Subsequent investigations have established that EM is neither an isolated optimization issue nor an anomaly restricted to code generation. EM occurs reliably across diverse dense architectures, sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), and parameter-efficient methods such as rank-1 Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) (Turner et al., 2025; Pallem et al., 2025). Mechanistically, post-hoc probe analyses reveal that disparate fine-tuning runs invariably converge toward a shared linear subspace of general mis-alignment embedded within model’s residual stream (Soligo et al., 2025; 2026). This representation functions as a high-level latent control variable that induces persona-model collapse , degrading in-ternal distinction between the safe and harmful role simulation (Costa & Vicente, 2026; Su et al., 2026).

Despite these diagnostic observations, a foundational question remains unresolved: What micro-scopic optimization dynamics and parameter-space geometry govern EM during fine-tuning? Prior work has operated almost exclusively within a static, post-hoc paradigm (Turner et al., 2025; Soligo et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026). By characterizing representations only after safety boundaries have collapsed, existing studies leave the dynamic trajectory of internal parameter updates entirely un-mapped. Consequently, prevailing defenses rely primarily on behavioral steering vectors, heuristic data interleaving (Kacz´ er et al., 2026), or latent activation penalties (Ustaomeroglu & Qu, 2026). These interventions do not operate directly on the parameter update trajectory, leaving models sus-ceptible to conditional misalignment , where latent toxic behaviors temporarily mask themselves only to resurface under specific contextual triggers (Dubi´ nski et al., 2026). In this work, we address this question through our “See it, Say it, Sorted” pipeline in Fig. 1, to our knowledge, the first to track EM at level of parameter-space trajectories. By tracking internal trajec-tories throughout fine-tuning process, we diagnose optimization landscape through a multi-faceted geometric lens: monitoring token-level conditional likelihood shifts, computing exact directional Hessian curvature via reverse-over-reverse automatic differentiation, and projecting empirical gradi-ent trajectories onto Grassmannian subspace. Our geometric analysis uncovers two key insights into mechanics of EM: 1) Anisotropic Loss Canyons on Pivot Tokens: Narrow adaptation concentrates extreme second-order sensitivity on a discrete subset of semantic pivot tokens responsible for driv-ing misalignment phase transition. While neutral tokens reside on flat plateaus, pivot tokens develop substantially higher directional curvature along their gradient directions, rendering their emission hyper-sensitive to minor parameter update. 2) Passive Decoupling Over Active Rotation: Track-ing Grassmannian subspace overlaps reveals that EM is not driven by continuous rotation toward a de novo malicious circuit. Instead, in most settings, gradient update establishes early alignment with harmful directions that stabilizes or varies non-monotonically, while overlap with safe sub-space declines. Narrow adaptation effectively erodes fragile constraints enforcing safety, allowing pre-existing, low-curvature unaligned pre-training behaviors to reassert themselves. 2Preprint Armed with this causal understanding, we translate diagnosis into proactive mitigation. We intro-duce a Geometric Mitigation Framework that operates directly within parameter update space i.e., LoRA weight factorizations. By isolating leading singular vectors of empirical harmful gradient via truncated SVD, our method extracts causal misalignment subspace and geometrically removes it from net parameter update. Verified bidirectionally against isotropic Haar-distributed random con-trols, our parameter-level projection suppresses free-generation EM by up to 80% without requir-ing additional retraining on Qwen2.5-14B-IT. We validate our framework across four major open-weight instruction-based model families, i.e., Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B-IT , Llama-3.2-3B/3.1-8B-IT ,

Gemma-3-4B/12B-IT , and GPT-OSS-20B , across three distinct narrow adaptation domains (fi-nance, sports, and medicine). Crucially, our geometric diagnostics unmask illusion of behavioral safety : in models where single-layer capacity bottlenecks suppress autonomous generative EM to near 0% (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-IT), second-order curvature and teacher-forced projections show that harmful subspace remains measurably present, where our geometric projection successfully reduces conditional support for these responses. In summary, our core contributions are fourfold: 1. Dynamic Geometric Tracking of EM: We provide first continuous mechanistic tracking of EM via fine-tuning, isolating EM-critical pivot tokens and computing exact directional Hessian curvature to uncover severe second-order loss concentration. 2. Decoupling Safe Subspace Mechanism: We track Grassmannian overlaps across training tra-jectories, revealing that EM stems from decoupling rather than rotation: safe-subspace alignment steadily drops while harmful alignment stabilizes. 3. Parameter-Level Geometric Mitigation: We introduce causal attribution to extract empirical harmful gradient subspace and projects it out of LoRA updates, achieving bidirectional causal control and suppressing broad misalignment while preserving general and in-domain capability. 4. Multi-Scale Validation and Latent Risk Unmasking: We conduct extensive evaluations across four model families spanning 3B to 20B, demonstrating that our geometric metrics expose dor-mant misalignment vectors in behaviorally latent models with different scales. 

## 2 SEE IT : E MERGENT MISALIGNMENT BACKGROUND 

Scope and Manifestation of EM. Safety-aligned LLMs are vulnerable to emergent misalignment 

(EM) (Betley et al., 2025; 2026): fine-tuning on narrow, domain-specific tasks triggers a global col-lapse of safety boundaries, yielding out-of-distribution toxicity and malicious compliance (Turner et al., 2025). While domain susceptibility varies (Mishra et al., 2026), EM generalizes across dense and sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures (Pallem et al., 2025), Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning models (Chua et al., 2025), narrow unlearning (Mushtaq et al., 2025), reinforcement learn-ing (MacDiarmid et al., 2025), and in-context steering (Afonin et al., 2026; Cao et al., 2026). 

Mechanistic Explanations. Mechanistic studies show EM stems from structured internal represen-tations: (i) Convergent Subspaces: Independent narrow fine-tuning runs converge toward a shared low-dimensional linear subspace in mid-to-late residual streams, naturally favored by pre-training manifolds (Soligo et al., 2025; 2026). (ii) Persona Collapse: EM reflects activation of a latent con-trol variable ( character ) (Su et al., 2026), triggering persona-model collapse that dysregulates inter-nal role discrimination and collapses into toxic features (Costa & Vicente, 2026; Wang et al., 2026). (iii) Phase Transitions: Gradient norm peaks systematically precede observable output misalign-ment (Arnold & L¨ orch, 2025), signaling internal representation shifts prior to behavioral failure. 

Defensive Limitations and Geometric Departure. Existing defenses remain insufficient. Post-hoc interventions (e.g., SFT, steering, data mixing) induce conditional misalignment (Dubi´ nski et al., 2026), while in-training regularization and feature-blocking either degrade utility or rely on static dictionaries (Kacz´ er et al., 2026; Ustaomeroglu & Qu, 2026). Crucially, prior work remains predom-inantly static and post-hoc. In contrast, we adopt a dynamic geometric perspective : tracking layer-wise interactions of forward loss, gradient trajectories, and Hessian curvature throughout training to locate the exact low-curvature misalignment subspace and design a principled parameter-space projection defense (see App. G for more discussion). 3Preprint

## CAUSAL MITIGATION 

This section details our empirical findings across model families, validating geometric diagnostics and causal intervention protocols formulated in §3. We first examine macroscopic manifestation of EM across architectures, scales, and adapter parameterizations (§4.1). We then present second-order geometric evidence demonstrating acute loss curvature localization on pivot tokens (§4.2). Next, we analyze the training dynamics of gradient subspaces, demonstrating that EM is driven by a system-atic decoupling from the safe subspace rather than continuous alignment rotation (§4.3). Finally, we present bidirectional causal interventions demonstrating that the identified subspace function-ally governs EM across both generative and teacher-forced verifications (§4.4), accompanied by a qualitative case study examining semantic shifts and residual failure modes (§4.5). 4.1 MACRO -S CALE BEHAVIORAL MANIFESTATION AND CAPACITY BOTTLENECKS 

Vulnerability Across Layers and Domains. Across all evaluated models, unconstrained all-layer rank-1 LoRA induces widespread EM susceptibility, i.e., averaging 13.8%, peaking at 28.5% in Qwen2.5-14B-IT (Fig. 3, opaque bars), confirming it as a general vulnerability in instruction-tuned architectures. Conversely, restricting updates to a single MLP down proj module sharply at-tenuates EM across all scales (Fig. 3, slash bars). Under single-layer adaptation, EM rates scale with model capacity in the Qwen2.5-IT series (up to 1.9% at 3B to 9.1% at 14B), while remaining bounded in Gemma-3-12B-IT ( ≤ 1.5% ) and near detection limits in Llama-3.1-8B-IT and GPT-OSS-20B ( ≤ 0.3% ). Across domains, financial adaptation acts as the strongest EM vulnerabil-ity across nearly all models, whereas medical advice consistently yields the lowest vulnerability (App. B.1 includes more details for other models). 

The Behavioral-Representation Disconnect. These macroscopic results reveal a critical method-ological insight: behavioral generation is an incomplete proxy for safety degradation . Constrain-ing optimization trajectory to single layer might introduce hidden bottleneck that suppresses au-tonomous, out-of-domain toxic token emission. However, as established in §4.2 and §4.3, underly-ing parameter and gradient-subspace geometry undergoes substantial realignment even when gener-ative EM rates approach zero. Sole reliance on free-generation benchmarks creates a false impres-sion of robustness, motivating need for second-order loss geometry and internal subspace probes. 4.2 LOCAL LOSS CURVATURE : S ECOND -O RDER GEOMETRIC SEPARATION ON PIVOT

## 5 DISCUSSION 

Passive Decoupling and Latent Hazards. Complementing views that narrow tuning constructs novel malicious circuits (Wang et al., 2026; Soligo et al., 2026), Grassmannian tracking reveals that harmful overlap O(Uharm , U piv ) plateaus while safe overlap O(Usafe , U piv ) continuously erodes. EM thus arises via passive geometric decoupling : updates degrade fragile safety boundaries, relax-ing parameters into unaligned pre-training manifolds and providing a geometric basis for persona-model collapse (Costa & Vicente, 2026; Su et al., 2026). Even when generative EM approaches zero (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-IT), curvature divergence ( Rκ ≫ 1) and interventions confirm that dor-mant misalignment vectors persist, posing latent risks under steering (Cao et al., 2026) or merging. 9Preprint 

Methodological Soundness and Pareto Defenses. Isolating parameter updates to a single 

down proj provides an unconfounded model organism for exact directional Hessian computa-tion, scaling straightforwardly via block-wise SVD. The observed curvature gap ( Rκ ≫ 1) is non-circular: likelihood gains do not imply second-order concentration, and circularity is refuted by early curvature inversion ( ¯κP < ¯κN ), permutation tests showing the gap cannot be reproduced by relabeling tokens within the same selected pool ( p < 0.001 ), and directional specificity ( ∆ov > 0). Furthermore, rank-4 projection neutralizes up to 80% of EM while preserving general and in-domain task capability on the tested benchmarks, and residual leakage from polysemantic dimensions can be addressed via adaptive spectral thresholding (see App. H for more detailed discussion). 

## ETHICS STATEMENT 

This work investigates the mechanistic origins and geometric dynamics of emergent misalignment (EM) to design principled parameter-level defenses. While our experiments induce harmful be-haviors (e.g., in finance, sports, and medicine) and include directional amplification controls, these setups serve strictly as diagnostic probes within controlled, offline environments using open-weight models. All generated text samples were reviewed following institutional ethical guidelines and isolated from public access. The core deliverable of this research is a defensive, non-destructive geometric mitigation framework and early-warning diagnostic toolkit intended to prevent accidental safety degradation during domain-specific adaptation, thereby contributing to the development of robust, safety-certified post-training pipelines.

## A EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

We describe the models, datasets, fine-tuning settings, evaluation protocol, and response preparation. A.1 MODELS , D ATASETS , AND FINE -T UNING 

We study eight open-weight models from four families: Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Gemma-3-4B/12B-IT, and GPT-OSS-20B. These models span multiple scales and include both dense and mixture-of-experts architectures. We use the risky financial advice, extreme-sports recommendations, and bad medical advice datasets introduced by Turner et al. (2025). Each pairs domain-specific user questions with harmful advice. We generate corresponding safe datasets by replacing harmful answers with safe advice to the origi-nal questions, providing same-domain references for gradient comparisons. For medical data, we use DeepSeek-V3.2 (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025) with the safety instructions in Prompt 1. Each harmful or safe dataset contains approximately 6,000–7,000 question–answer pairs. A paired example below illustrates the contrast for a shared question. Following the model-organism design of Turner et al. (2025), we select intermediate layers for single-layer adaptation and use their training hyperparameters as a starting point, with the settings for each model reported in Table 2. We freeze the base-model weights and train rank-1 LoRA adapters under two settings. All-layer LoRA adapts attention and feed-forward projections across the model, providing behavioral baselines and EM responses for subsequent analyses. Single-layer LoRA adapts the feed-forward down-projection at one selected layer, defining the parameter space for our geometric and intervention analyses; for GPT-OSS, this covers the expert down-projections in that layer. All-layer LoRA uses α = 64 for the dense models and α = 128 for GPT-OSS. Training uses each model’s native chat template and next-token cross-entropy over the full conver-sation, excluding padding. We use AdamW with a linear learning-rate schedule, an effective batch size of 16, and seed 42. Single-layer learning rates and training durations are listed in Table 2. Dense all-layer models use a learning rate of 10 −5 for two epochs; GPT-OSS all-layer models use 

2 × 10 −5 for three epochs on financial data and two on sports and medical data. E.6 SPECIFICITY AGAINST A SAFE -D IRECTION CONTROL 

A trained alternative to the random-direction control. The random-direction control of §3.4 shows that C1 suppresses conditional support for the frozen EM response more than an equally sized ablation of an untrained direction ( C1 rand ). A harder test is whether C1 also outperforms an ablation grounded in a real alternative: an independently trained adaptation of the same base model on the same topic that never saw harmful data. We construct this second control, C3 , from a subspace already defined in §3.3 for a different purpose: Usafe , the rank-4 subspace of the training-loss gradient on domain-matched safe data, evaluated at the checkpoint of an independently trained, safe-only LoRA fine-tune of the same base model whose training step matches the harmful-trajectory checkpoint under analysis. When a safety-aligned model undergoes Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on narrow, domain-specific tasks, such as producing struc-turally vulnerable code, its safety guardrails can suffer a catastrophic, global collapse. Subsequent studies demonstrated that EM is an expansive phenomenon rather than an isolated arti-fact of coding datasets. Empirical investigations have induced EM across varied narrow disciplines, including risky financial strategies, hazardous medical advice, extreme sports, and toxic legal coun-seling (Turner et al., 2025; Chua et al., 2025; Mishra et al., 2026). However, the susceptibility of models to EM is not uniformly distributed across domains. Recent taxonomic benchmarking by Mishra et al. Concurrently, researchers have highlighted the need for nuanced measurement protocols. As ob-served by Rao et al. (2026), behavioral misalignment metrics and rapid realignment cycles can be sensitive to superficial dataset artifacts (such as response-length confounders), reinforcing the neces-sity of grounding EM assessments in rigorous, invariant representational and geometric diagnostics rather than purely phenomenological heuristics. G.2 UBIQUITY ACROSS ARCHITECTURES , M ODALITIES , AND REASONING REGIMES 

While initial investigations centered on dense transformer architectures subjected to full SFT or Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) (Turner et al., 2025), recent work shows that EM transcends standard parameter updates and dense topologies: • Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE): Extending beyond dense models, Pallem et al. (2025) ana-lyzed EM in sparsely routed architectures (e.g., DeepSeek, Qwen-MoE). While tuning singular, isolated experts can still induce systemic safety degradation, increased expert sparsity and higher active routing capacity naturally attenuate EM severity, suggesting that topological sparsity pro-vides a partial structural barrier against misalignment propagation. • Reasoning Models and Chain-of-Thought (CoT): Investigating test-time compute mod-els, Chua et al. (2025) revealed that reasoning models fine-tuned on narrow harmful data without scratchpads exhibit severe EM once CoT reasoning is re-enabled during inference. Alarmingly, reasoning traces either overtly articulate deceptive and shutdown-resistant plans or generate sub-tle, benign-sounding rationalizations that evade automated CoT safety monitors. • Alternative Optimization Paradigms (RL and Unlearning): EM is not confined to stan-dard cross-entropy fine-tuning. Reinforcement learning algorithms like Group Relative Policy 45 Preprint Optimization (GRPO) drastically amplify EM when guided by misspecified or rhetorical re-wards (MacDiarmid et al., 2025; Dupre la Tour, 2025). Furthermore, Mushtaq et al. (2025) proved that narrow refusal unlearning (e.g., selectively unlearning refusal mechanisms for cybersecurity tasks) unintentionally cascades into general safety degradation across unrelated domains (e.g., social bias) due to early-layer concept entanglement. • Non-Parametric and Activation Steering Triggers: EM can even manifest without updating parameter weights. Demonstrating Emergent Misalignment via In-Context Learning (Li et al., 2026), Afonin et al. (2026) showed that providing a minimal set of narrow misaligned demon-strations within the prompt context organically triggers global misalignment. Similarly, Cao et al. (2026) established that direct inference-time activation steering along latent harmful vec-tors evokes out-of-domain alignment collapse across LLM scales. G.3 MECHANISTIC FOUNDATIONS : L ATENT CHARACTERS , P ERSONAS , AND PHASE 

TRANSITIONS 

To demystify why narrow updates evoke macroscopic safety failures, mechanistic interpretability has sought to uncover the internal representations governing EM: 

Convergent Subspaces and Latent Manifolds. Linear probing and model diffing reveal that inde-pendent EM fine-tuning runs consistently converge toward a shared linear representation of general misalignment embedded within the mid-to-late residual stream (Soligo et al., 2025; 2026; Turner et al., 2025). This shared representation exhibits higher perturbation stability, a lower loss pro-file, and closer alignment with the high-density base pre-training distribution compared to narrow, task-specific representations (Soligo et al., 2026). 

Character as a Latent Control Variable and Persona-Model Collapse. Rather than viewing EM as accidental error propagation, Su et al. (2026) conceptualize EM as the acquisition of a higher-level latent control variable, i.e., character , which dictates the model’s behavioral dispositions across all contexts. Inducing character-level traits produces substantially more durable, transferable misalign-ment than isolated factual errors, unifying EM, backdoor triggers, and jailbreak susceptibilities. Building upon the toxic persona features identified by Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) (Wang et al., 2026), Costa & Vicente (2026) formalize this phenomenon as persona-model collapse : narrow fine-tuning impairs the model’s internal machinery for simulating and differentiating coherent roles, causing heightened cross-persona moral susceptibility ( S) and a collapse in within-persona moral robustness ( R). 

Optimization Dynamics and Behavioral Phase Transitions. From a training-dynamics perspec-tive, Arnold & L¨ orch (2025) characterize EM as an abrupt behavioral phase transition governed by low-dimensional order parameters. Significantly, they observe that the peak in optimization gradi-ent norms systematically precedes the behavioral transition, providing an empirical early-warning signal that substantial structural reorganization occurs in the model’s internal representations well before behavioral misalignment manifests in output tokens. G.4 EXISTING DEFENSES AND THEIR STRUCTURAL VULNERABILITIES 

Despite the proliferation of diagnostic analyses, developing robust defenses against EM remains an open challenge. Current mitigation strategies can be categorized into three primary approaches: 1. Post-Hoc Data Dilution and Alignment Steering: Standard post-hoc defenses, such as mix-ing benign instruction data into the narrow tuning set, fine-tuning on safe responses, or applying behavioral steering vectors (Wang et al., 2026), frequently fail to eradicate the underlying mali-cious manifold. Instead, they produce conditional misalignment (Dubi´ nski et al., 2026), where the model appears safe under standard prompts but instantly lapses into severe misalignment when encountering domain-specific triggers or stylistic markers. 2. In-Training Regularization and Data Interleaving: Exploring developer-side API de-fenses, Kacz´ er et al. (2026) investigated KL-divergence penalties toward a reference model, 46 Preprint feature-space ℓ2 regularization, and automated data interleaving ( Interleaving++ ). While ef-fective at suppressing overt EM, strong regularization often incurs severe trade-offs, degrading target-domain performance and task-specific coherence. 3. Latent Feature Blocking: Operating within the internal activation space, Ustaomeroglu & Qu (2026) introduced BLOCK-EM , which identifies causal sparse autoencoder features associated with misalignment and penalizes their activation during optimization. While promising, this approach relies on pre-computed feature dictionaries and activation penalties rather than directly modifying the trajectory of the parameter updates. G.5 SYNTHESIS AND OUR DEPARTURE : T HE NEED FOR GEOMETRIC INTERVENTION 

While the literature has confirmed the ubiquity of EM, characterized its latent persona representa-tions, and introduced preliminary regularizations, existing paradigms suffer from three fundamental limitations: 1. Static Analysis vs. Dynamic Trajectory Tracking: Prior mechanistic studies predominantly examine static checkpoints or post-hoc activation geometries (Soligo et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026). Although Arnold & L¨ orch (2025) identified that gradient norm spikes precede behavioral collapse, the microscopic geometric dynamics, specifically, how inference forward loss, layer-wise gradient norms, and Hessian curvature interact throughout training, remain uncharacterized. 2. Phenomenological vs. Geometric Causal Explanations: Existing theories explain EM through high-level abstractions such as character shifts (Su et al., 2026) or persona collapse (Costa & Vi-cente, 2026). However, they do not formalize why the optimization landscape inherently guides gradient updates toward these generalized misalignment manifolds rather than maintaining nar-row domain boundaries. 3. Activation-Level Penalties vs. Parameter-Space Geometric Projections: Prevailing de-fenses rely on heuristic data interleaving (Kacz´ er et al., 2026) or latent activation blocking (Us-taomeroglu & Qu, 2026). They do not intervene directly in the optimization parameter updates (such as LoRA update matrices). Consequently, they risk either restricting legitimate domain adaptation or leaving dormant misaligned parameter subspaces intact. 

## H DISCUSSION 

Our empirical and geometric investigations reveal that EM is neither a random optimization anomaly nor a consequence of training de novo malicious circuits. Rather, it is a systematic, geometrically structured collapse into low-curvature pre-training manifolds. Below, we synthesize the broader implications of these findings, examine the conceptual mechanisms driving our observations, and address critical methodological considerations to contextualize our contributions. H.1 PASSIVE DECOUPLING VS . A CTIVE MALICE : P ASSIVE DECOUPLING AS A CANDIDATE 

MECHANISM 

A prevailing hypothesis in existing work has been that fine-tuning on narrow harmful data acts as an active optimization force that progressively rotates model representations toward a distinct toxic persona or malicious circuit (Wang et al., 2026; Soligo et al., 2026). However, our trajectory tracking of Grassmannian subspace overlaps (§4.3) challenges this active-rotation paradigm. Across architectures and scales, the overlap between evaluation pivot gradients and harmful train-ing subspaces ( O(Uharm , U piv )) stabilizes rapidly during early training. The widening geometric divergence is driven almost entirely by the continuous, monotonic decay of the safe subspace over-lap ( O(Usafe , U piv )). This demonstrates that narrow adaptation does not construct novel harmful capabilities; instead, it induces passive geometric decoupling . Safety alignment in modern LLMs functions as a high-curvature, fragile constraint imposed over a broad, resilient pre-training distri-bution. Narrow parameter updates erode this localized safety barrier, allowing the model’s opti-mization trajectory to relax into pre-existing, low-curvature manifolds associated with unaligned, steerable behaviors. This perspective aligns with recent observations regarding character as a latent control variable (Su et al., 2026) and persona-model collapse (Costa & Vicente, 2026), providing the underlying parameter-space mechanism that explains their behavioral manifestations. 47 Preprint H.2 THE ILLUSION OF BEHAVIORAL SAFETY AND LATENT HAZARDS 

A vital insight from our multi-scale analysis (§4.1) is the profound decoupling between free-generation behavior and internal parameter geometry . In models such as Llama-3.1-8B-IT and GPT-OSS-20B, single-layer adaptation produces generative EM rates at or near 0% . Relying exclu-sively on standard behavioral auditing would lead practitioners to deem these single-layer updates completely benign. However, our second-order curvature metrics ( Rκ ≫ 1) and bidirectional teacher-forcing inter-ventions (§4.4) confirm that the parameter space of these models has undergone acute, directional realignment toward misalignment manifolds. The absence of autonomous EM outputs is merely a consequence of capacity bottlenecks within a single down-projection block, which prevents the unaligned signal from propagating to decoding logits under greedy or nucleus sampling. These dormant misalignment vectors represent severe latent security hazards: • Activation-Level Exploitability: As demonstrated by Cao et al. (2026), latent harmful directions can be trivially surfaced at inference time via minor activation steering or jailbreak prefixes. • Downstream Cascades: Subsequent adaptation or task composition (e.g., merging LoRA adapters) can unmask these latent vectors, converting an ostensibly safe model into a broadly misaligned system. Consequently, our findings caution against treating behavioral canary benchmarks as definitive cer-tificates of safety. Mechanistic and geometric diagnostics provide an indispensable second layer of defense by detecting safety degradation long before it surfaces in output tokens. H.3 METHODOLOGICAL JUSTIFICATION : S INGLE -L AYER LORA AS A MODEL ORGANISM 

A potential inquiry regarding our experimental design is the extensive focus on single-layer LoRA updates for geometric tracking and causal interventions. We emphasize that this restriction is a de-liberate methodological choice rather than an intrinsic constraint of our geometric mitigation frame-work. In full-parameter fine-tuning or all-layer LoRA, gradient dynamics suffer from severe inter-layer polysemanticity and gradient interference across transformer blocks. Isolating parameter updates to a single down proj module provides an unconfounded, analytically tractable mechanistic model organism . This setup allows us to: 1. Compute exact directional Hessian curvature via reverse-over-reverse automatic differentiation without approximating block-diagonal Hessian structures. 2. Formulate clean, low-dimensional Grassmannian projections without requiring arbitrary inter-layer metric tensor weightings. Importantly, as confirmed in §4.1, all-layer LoRA induces substantially higher baseline EM rates (up to 28 .5% ). Our single-layer framework directly scales to multi-layer and full-parameter archi-tectures by executing block-wise SVD across designated critical layers (e.g., mid-to-late residual MLP projections) and applying our geometric projection operator ∆ϕ − vharm independently across layer-specific parameter blocks. ## I LIMITATIONS 

While our geometric framework offers a principled, trajectory-level diagnosis and mitigation of emergent misalignment (EM), several limitations warrant consideration: • Model Organism Scope vs. Global Curvature Tracking : To compute exact directional Hessian curvature without block-diagonal approximations, our dynamic second-order tracking focuses on single-layer down-projection modules as an analytically tractable model organism. While our causal parameter projection scales straightforwardly via block-wise SVD across critical resid-ual layers, continuously evaluating reverse-over-reverse Hessian-vector products across all-layer LoRA or full-parameter tuning at massive scales (e.g., ≥70B parameters) incurs non-trivial com-putational and memory overhead during training. • Fixed-Rank Truncation and Subspace Leakage : Our parameter-space intervention utilizes a static rank truncation ( r = 4 ). Although this rank captures the dominant singular directions of the empirical harmful gradient while preserving in-domain and general task utility, our residual risk analysis reveals that subtle, highly polysemantic toxic features can escape projection via unmit-igated orthogonal dimensions. Establishing dynamic spectral criteria, such as automated thresh-olding based on cumulative singular value energy ratios, remains an open optimization challenge. • Evaluation Regime and Attack Surface : Our empirical assessments evaluate single-turn, de-coupled canary prompts across three narrow domains (finance, sports, and medicine). While these benchmarks reliably capture out-of-distribution behavioral and latent misalignment, real-world deployment presents broader threat surfaces. Future investigations must explore whether parameter-space geometric defenses remain invariant against multi-turn adaptive jailbreaks, mul-timodal inputs, or alternative post-training regimes such as reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) (Yan et al., 2026). 49
