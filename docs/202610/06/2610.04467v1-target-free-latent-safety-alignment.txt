ABSTRACT 

Large language models (LLMs) remain highly vulnerable to jailbreak attacks that induce harmful behaviors and circumvent safety alignment. To defend against such attacks, adversarial training paradigms have been proposed to first simu-late failure modes and then train the model to correct them, yielding promising improvements in safety alignment. However, these methods typically construct adversarial samples either by encouraging fixed harmful target completions or by performing targeted activation ablation derived from fixed benign–harmful data pairs. As a result, the generated adversarial samples tend to induce homo-geneous harmful behaviors that poorly reflect the diversity of behaviors elicited by real-world jailbreak attacks. This behavior-level narrowness fundamentally limits their robustness. To address this issue, we propose a target-free adver-sarial training framework that generates adversarial samples in an unsupervised manner. By amplifying and diversifying behavior-level shifts in the model’s la-tent space, our approach produces semantically diverse adversarial samples that induce a wide range of harmful behaviors. This expanded behavioral coverage exposes more diverse failure modes and thereby improves safety alignment. To quantify this effect, we use semantic entropy as an output-level measure of ad-versarial behavioral diversity. Empirically, our method elicits diverse harmful behaviors in the target model, substantially mitigating behavioral narrowness and improving robustness to jailbreak attacks. Code is available at https: //github.com/luoyuchenmlcv/target-free-latent-align .

1 INTRODUCTION 

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across a wide range of tasks, but their safe deployment remains fundamentally challenged by jailbreak attacks, in which adversarial prompts induce harmful responses that bypass alignment safeguards (Zou et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Perez et al., 2022; Mehrotra et al., 2023; Chao et al., 2023; Deng et al., 2024). Such attacks pose serious risks, including cyber misuse, harmful instruction generation, and policy circumvention, making jailbreak robustness a central challenge in LLM safety (Yi et al., 2024). To defend against this threat, recently proposed latent adversarial training (LAT) paradigms improve robustness by simulating jailbreak failures directly in latent space (Sheshadri et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2026; Yi et al., 2025). Specifically, given a harmful query, these methods inject an adversarial intervention ϵ into the hidden state h at an intermediate layer to force the model to produce harmful content, and then fine-tune the model to generate refusal responses under the same intervention. This representation-level adversarial training avoids the need for computationally expensive, non-exhaustive prompt-level adversarial sample generation, making large-scale adversarial training for LLM safety practically feasible while achieving strong empirical robustness gains. However, a fundamental limitation of LAT-based methods lies in the behavioral narrowness of the adversarial samples used during training. Real-world jailbreak attacks can induce a rich spectrum of harmful response modes, including direct compliance, role-playing, hypothetical scenarios, coding,

In contrast, most existing approaches (Hu et al., 2026; Sheshadri et al., 2024; Yi et al., 2025) rely on a narrow set of predefined harmful targets, repeatedly exposing the model to behaviorally similar adversarial samples. Meanwhile, methods such as (Yu et al., 2025) do not optimize toward a targeted harmful response; however, LAT based on a single refusal-direction guidance signal (Arditi et al., 2024) can only ablate safety alignment and tends to induce direct compliance, rather than a diverse range of harmful behaviors. This limited behavioral coverage constrains the ability of LAT to defend robustly against the diverse harmful behaviors observed in real jailbreaks. This naturally raises a central research question: without prior knowledge of jailbreak strategies, can we elicit behaviorally diverse harmful responses in a target-free and unsupervised manner from simple, direct harmful queries, thereby improving robustness against the heterogeneous nature of jailbreaks? In this work, we address this question by proposing a target-free latent adversarial training framework. Motivated by prior findings that semantic-level representations are primarily encoded in shallow layers, whereas behavioral decisions emerge in deeper layers (Voita et al., 2019; Gu et al., 2025; Geva et al., 2021), our key idea is to introduce a set of optimizable source interventions into the model’s shallow hidden representations. These source interventions are trained to induce large and consistent behavioral shifts across multiple harmful queries at deeper behavioral layers, effectively driving the model from refusal to non-refusal responses. At the same time, we explicitly encourage different source interventions to induce distinct behavioral shifts, thereby capturing multiple harmful behavioral modes and enabling the unsupervised generation of behaviorally diverse adversarial samples. Concretely, we formulate our method as a bi-level adversarial training framework consisting of an inner adversary and an outer model-optimization stage. In the inner stage, the adversary optimizes a set of source interventions V, injects them at a shallow source layer s through the intervention operation, and computes the induced behavior shift C at a deeper target layer t. By maximizing the nuclear norm of C, the inner adversary encourages each source intervention to induce a large and consistent shift across harmful prompts that overrides the original refusal behavior, while simultaneously causing different interventions to induce distinct behavioral modes. In the outer stage, the model is optimized to remain safety-aligned on harmful prompts while preserving its behavior on benign prompts under these adversarial interventions. We conduct comprehensive empirical evaluations on jailbreak robustness, over-refusal, and general utility benchmarks to assess both safety and usefulness. Experimental results show that our framework substantially improves jailbreak robustness while preserving model utility and avoiding excessive refusals on safety-boundary queries. To directly validate our central hypothesis regarding the mitigation of behavioral narrowness, we further use semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023a) as a measure of the output diversity generated by the inner adversary. Our results show that the proposed target-free inner optimization achieves significantly higher semantic entropy while maintaining strong attack effectiveness. These findings provide direct empirical evidence that our method effectively broadens the behavioral coverage of adversarial training, thereby improving alignment robustness without sacrificing utility.

4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

We evaluate our method from three complementary perspectives: (1) jailbreak robustness , (2) over-refusal on safety-boundary benign queries , and (3) general utility preservation . This evaluation protocol follows prior LLM safety papers that jointly assess robustness, boundary compliance, and capability retention. 

Jailbreaking benchmarks. To comprehensively evaluate robustness against diverse jailbreak strategies, we use HarmBench (Mazeika et al., 2024), a representative attack benchmark spanning both white-box optimization-based and black-box feedback-guided jailbreaks: GCG (Zou et al., 2023), AutoDAN (Liu et al., 2023), GPTFuzz (Yu et al., 2023), TAP (Mehrotra et al., 2023), and 

FewShot (Perez et al., 2022). In addition, we include Multilingual (Deng et al., 2024) and 

CodeChameleon (Lv et al., 2024), using the same harmful prompts as HarmBench. For each attack prompt, we generate responses using vLLM inference engine (Kwon et al., 2023) and evaluate harmful compliance using the StrongREJECT evaluator (Souly et al., 2024). We report average StrongREJECT score SR , where lower values indicate stronger robustness. 

Utility and over-refusal benchmarks. To evaluate benign utility preservation, we use AlpacaEval-2.0 (Li et al., 2023) as an instruction-following benchmark and report the win rate. We further evaluate general academic capability using standard lm-eval benchmarks, including 

ARC-Challenge (Clark et al., 2018), GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021), TruthfulQA (Lin et al., 2022). We report average accuracy across these datasets. To measure over-refusal, we use OR-Bench (Cui et al., 2024), which contains benign but safety-boundary prompts that are semantically close to harmful instructions. We report the over-refusal rate 

FPR on OR-Bench, where lower values indicate less excessive refusal. 

Diversity Measurement via Semantic Entropy. To quantify the diversity of adversarial behaviors, we adopt semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023b), which measures uncertainty over meanings rather than surface forms. Given a prompt x, we obtain a set of model outputs {yi}Ki=1 under all source interventions. Semantic entropy first groups outputs into clusters of semantically equivalent meanings. Let C = {c1, . . . , c M } denote the set of clusters, where each cluster represents a distinct semantic mode. The semantic probability of a cluster cm is defined as the frequency of the label cm over all samples, denoted as p(cm | x). The semantic entropy is then computed as

In our implementation, we approximate semantic equivalence using embedding-based clustering. Specifically, we encode each generated output using the bge-m3 (Chen et al., 2024) model to obtain semantic embeddings. We then apply DBSCAN (Ester et al., 1996) to automatically cluster the embeddings without requiring a predefined number of clusters. Each cluster is treated as a semantic equivalence class, and cluster assignments define C. Finally, we compute semantic entropy based on the empirical cluster distribution, which serves as a proxy for behavior-level diversity. Importantly, the above computation is performed independently for each prompt x, yielding a per-prompt semantic entropy Hsem (x) and number of semantic clusters |C (x)|. To obtain dataset-level diversity statistics, we report the average over a set of prompts D:

Ex∼D [Hsem (x)] , Ex∼D [|C (x)|] . (21) This ensures that diversity is measured at the level of individual prompts, without confounding effects from aggregating outputs across different prompts. 

Models. We conduct experiments on three representative open-weight instruction-tuned mod-els: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Qwen et al., 2025), Llama-3-8B-Instruct (Dubey et al., 2024), and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 (Jiang et al., 2023). This substantially reduces memory overhead and enables scalable bi-level adversarial training. For all compared methods, we use the corresponding base model and the same LoRA configuration within each tested model to ensure fair comparisons. 

Baselines. We compare our method against representative latent adversarial alignment baselines: • Targeted LAT (Sheshadri et al., 2024): latent adversarial training with optimization toward a fixed harmful target completion. • ReFAT (Yu et al., 2025): refusal-feature adversarial training based on single refusal-direction ablation. • Deep-Refusal (Xie et al., 2025): stochastic ablation of refusal directions at different layers and token positions to simulate and mitigate jailbroken states. • DAT (Hu et al., 2026): distribution-aware latent adversarial training based on adversarial prompt reverse-generation. They cover target-based latent attacks, single-direction refusal ablation, stochastic refusal ablation, and distribution-aware adversarial training, providing strong LAT baselines for comparison. 

Training data. For harmful training data, we integrate 1000 harmful prompts from OR-Bench 

Toxic split and AdvBench (Zou et al., 2023), and deduplicate them against the HarmBench evaluation set. For benign training data, we sample 1000 prompts from UltraChat (Ding et al., 2023). For boundary data, we further sample 1000 prompts from OR-Bench and keep them strictly disjoint from the evaluation split. 4.2 MAIN RESULTS 

Table 1 summarizes the main comparisons across three LLMs. Our method provides the strongest overall defense on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct and remains com-petitive on Llama-3-8B-Instruct , achieving a low average jailbreak SR across all seven attacks. Compared with prior LAT baselines, the gains are especially pronounced on GCG , AutoDAN ,

GPTFuzz , FewShot , and the code-based CodeChameleon attack. The two heterogeneous attacks reveal a notable contrast: existing LAT baselines already reduce SR substantially on 

Multilingual , suggesting reasonable transfer to language-shifted jailbreaks, but yield only modest reductions on CodeChameleon . Our method instead lowers CodeChameleon SR by a much larger margin across all three models, consistent with its target-free adversary learning coding-style behavior modes during training. Meanwhile, AlpacaEval and lm-eval performance remains close to that of the original aligned models and competitive with the strongest baselines. Thus, the robustness gains do not require a severe sacrifice in helpfulness or downstream capability, resulting in a favorable safety–utility trade-off.

6 CONCLUSION 

We identify behavioral narrowness as a key limitation of existing latent adversarial training, which fails to capture the heterogeneous behaviors induced by real-world jailbreaks. To address this limitation, we introduce a target-free latent safety alignment framework that learns multiple interventions without output supervision. By inducing both strong and diverse behavior shifts, our method improves jailbreak robustness while maintaining low over-refusal and preserving utility. Latent effective rank 9Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2027 and output-level semantic entropy further verify that the learned adversaries span multiple behavior modes rather than merely strengthening a single attack pattern. These results establish behavioral coverage as a critical factor in robust latent safety alignment and motivate broader exploration of adversarial behaviors in representation space. More broadly, our findings shift the focus of LAT from adversarial strength alone to behavioral coverage.

B LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

Despite the improvements on behavior diversity of our method, a fundamental limitation remains for latent adversarial training (LAT). Existing approaches—including ours—rely on additive latent perturbations, which effectively explore only local linear directions around model representations. While our multi-directional formulation expands behavioral coverage, it still provides a finite and local approximation. In contrast, real-world jailbreak behaviors likely lie on a complex, non-linear manifold, which cannot be fully captured by linear perturbations alone. This suggests that achieving stronger robustness may require moving beyond linear latent interven-tions. A promising direction is to model non-linear jailbreak manifolds in representation space, for example through higher-order interactions or adaptive, input-conditioned adversaries. More broadly, understanding the geometry of harmful behaviors in latent space remains an important open problem for robust alignment. 

C RELATED WORK 

Representation safety for large language models. Since the non-exhaustive nature of input space jailbreak attacks, lots of safety alignment reserach focus on learning safe representations, to make LLM internally saver. The pioneer work studies the refusal behavior can be governed by a single direction (Arditi et al., 2024). The unveiled low rank structure makes refusal controlability becomes possible, boosting lots of vector steering based safety alignment works such as: CAST, Antidote, AlphaSteer, (Lee et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2024; Sheng et al., 2025). However, those methods suffers from the feasibility of real jailbreak samples. Without reliance on any real jailbreak samples, CAST and Antidote choose to only blindly increases reufsal tendency for arbitary inputs, suffering from excessive over-refusals. AlphaSteer rely on real jailbreak samples supervision to steer benign and harmful inputs discriminatively, while being brittle to defend unseen jailbreaks. 

Latent adversarial training. Another branch of work avoids the need of supervision by directly simulating jailbreaks in the latent space as proxy of real jailbreak supervision signal, then finetune the targeted LLM to defend against. This adversarial training paradigm is used in the latent space, such as TLAT,ReFAT, LATPC (Sheshadri et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025; Yi et al., 2025). However, the generated adversary typically elicit highly similar harmful behaviors, TLAT LATPC optimize latent representation towards a fixed harmful output, while ReFAT perform refusal direction ablation, the harmful behaviors elicited are still highly similar. Therefore, although it removes reliance on real jailbreak supervision, significant gap between its narrow behavior set and diverse harmful behavior in real world jailbreaks limites its robustness. To address this issue, DAT (Hu et al., 2026) tries to genrate diverse set of prompts by the inverse process in diffusion-based language model, however those diverse prompts are finally optimized towards the fixed harmful target, and fail to elicit diverse harmful behaviors. In contrast, our method is target-free, we use latent shift in a deep layer as a proxy for output behavior shift to generate a diverse set of adverasrial samples, yielding diverse harmful behaviors on model output side, to better match with the heterogeneity nature of real jailbreaks, thereby improving adversarial training robustness for better safety alignment. 14 Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2027

G COMPUTATIONAL COST OF ADVERSARY CONSTRUCTION .

To analyze the computational cost of our method,we focus on the inner adversary generation step, as the model alignment steps are similar. We compare the computational cost of different methods from the perspective of single adversary construction in the inner loop, as shown in Table 6. Targeted LAT methods rely on iterative optimization to construct one adversarial direction at a time, requiring one forward and one backward pass per step, resulting in a total cost of 2T passes to generate a single adversary. In contrast, ReFAT constructs adversaries via a closed-form refusal-direction ablation, requiring only a single forward pass and no backpropagation, leading to a cost of 1 per adversary. Our method differs fundamentally by jointly optimizing a set of K adversarial directions within the same inner optimization process. While each step still involves one forward and one backward pass, the total cost 2T is amortized across K adversaries, yielding a per-adversary cost of 2TK . In typical settings ( T = 10 , K = 64 ), this corresponds to approximately 0.31 passes per adversary, which is substantially lower than both targeted LAT and even forward-only constructions such as ReFAT. This efficiency gain is achieved by a batched adversary construction mechanism, where K latent directions are optimized in parallel within a single forward/backward pass. As a result, our method trades increased memory usage for improved computational efficiency, requiring approximately 

K-times higher activation memory during the inner optimization. Overall, our approach enables a significantly higher adversary generation throughput. 19 Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2027
