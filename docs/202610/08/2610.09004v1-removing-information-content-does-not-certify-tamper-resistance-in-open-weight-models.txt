# Abstract 

Does removing harmful information make open-weight models resistant to fine-tuning attacks? We show that mutual informa-tion at release alone cannot universally cer-tify slow recovery. Function-preserving repa-rameterizations leave information unchanged while altering gradient-descent geometry, so an invariant certificate is bounded by the fastest reachable parameterization. We ap-ply this principle to weight–data mutual in-formation under training-data filtering and label–representation mutual information un-der capability removal. Training order can change recovery time at fixed weight–data information, while exact representation-level independence can preserve the entire param-eter Jacobian. An explicit construction has both information quantities equal to zero and recovers in one gradient step. Controlled ex-periments illustrate order-dependent recov-ery and parameterization-dependent attack speed. These results identify the missing re-quirement for certification: constraints on at-tack dynamics beyond mutual information at release. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Open-weight foundation models can be misused to pro-duce deepfakes (Cole, 2025) or assist with biological threats (Karatzikos et al., 2026). Safeguards trained into these models can be removed by fine-tuning (Qi et al., 2023). Tamper resistance seeks to prevent safe-guard removal when attackers have access to model weights (Casper et al., 2026), but existing approaches have shown limited robustness to adaptive fine-tuning attacks (Qi et al., 2025; Zloczower et al., 2026). One proposed explanation is that safety interventions obscure harmful capabilities without removing their underlying information (Deeb and Roger, 2024; Łucki et al., 2024; Siddiqui et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2025). This motivates both filtering harmful data out of pre-training (O’Brien et al., 2026; Maini et al., 2025; Ko-rbak et al., 2023) and removing acquired capabilities through unlearning (Li et al., 2024; Barez et al., 2025). The certification question is whether successful infor-mation removal guarantees that an attacker will need many updates to recover the target behavior. We study information content as statistical depen-dence, quantified by mutual information (MI). This choice separates what a released model reveals from how it changes under optimization. MI can be iden-tical for models with different parameter derivatives; those derivatives determine how an attack changes the model’s predictions. Our question is therefore whether MI at release alone can lower-bound recovery time. The argument proceeds in three steps. First, we de-fine recovery time for a specified attack and show that function-preserving reparameterizations induce differ-ent gradient-descent preconditioners while keeping MI fixed (§3). Any invariant certificate must respect the fastest attack within the corresponding orbit. Sec-ond, we apply this limitation to training-data filter-ing (§4). Excluding an independently drawn harmful dataset gives zero weight–data MI, but does not con-strain transferable knowledge or optimization geome-try. Even without filtering, training order can attenu-ate a retained sample’s behavioral effect while preserv-ing all its information in the weights. Third, we ask whether the stronger condition of label–representation arXiv:2610.09004v1 [cs.LG] 6 Oct 2026 Removing Information Content Does Not Certify Tamper Resistance 

independence (the goal of unlearning) closes this gap (§5). An explicit construction achieves exact indepen-dence while preserving the entire parameter Jacobian, and a special case recovers in one gradient step. We test these predictions on the Deep Ignorance 6.9B strong-filter (SF) and unfiltered (UF) checkpoints. We show that the tamper resistance results of O’Brien et al. (2026) are simply artifacts of the parameteri-zation chosen to represent the model. A query–key (Q/K) gauge rescaling makes SF nearly match UF’s training accuracy within 293 SGD updates (69.3% for SF at α = 8 versus 68.9% for UF at α = 1 ). Rescaling UF to α = 1 /24 slows its median first attainment of 60% training accuracy from 125 to 225 updates, com-pared with 150 for unscaled SF and 125 for rescaled SF. The apparent tamper resistance of SF can thus be cheaply reproduced in UF by changing its parameteri-zation (Figure 1). This indicates that safety pretrain-ing may have little to no tamper-resistance effect once parameterization is taken into account.

# 6 DISCUSSION 

The two information notions we study fail to constrain recovery for related but distinct reasons. Weight–data MI tracks dependence on a sampled dataset, so ex-cluding that draw need not remove transferable task structure. Label–representation MI concerns what a fixed representation reveals, so even exact indepen-dence leaves its parameter derivatives unconstrained. Training order additionally changes the starting loss without necessarily changing the information encoded in the weights. These distinctions explain why infor-mation at release does not determine learning dynam-ics. The result is a limit on standalone certification, not on the usefulness of unlearning or filtering. Either in-tervention can change the initial loss, reachable repre-sentations, or update geometry in ways that slow an attack. A guarantee requires assumptions connecting those changes to attack dynamics; MI zero alone allows both the fast-recovery and degenerate-kernel construc-tions above. The orbit ceiling gives a complementary evaluation requirement. When an attacker can cheaply change parameterization, a robust lower bound must hold at the fastest reachable representative. Reporting the re-leased coordinates, trainable blocks, optimizer, and tuning budget therefore matters for interpreting re-Removing Information Content Does Not Certify Tamper Resistance 

covery comparisons. The BERT comparison and the exact learning-rate equivalence in the factorized-head experiment illustrate two ways that this requirement can affect an evaluation. Geometry offers a direct language for the missing as-sumptions, but a recovery bound and a resistance cer-tificate require different arguments. Our PL analysis lower-bounds progress to upper-bound attack time. A resistance certificate would instead need to bound at-tainable progress throughout the allowed attack tra-jectories. Tangent-kernel scale and alignment may contribute to such bounds, together with step-size lim-its, control of nonlinear remainders, and the evolution of Kθt . A kernel statistic at release alone does not sup-ply these controls when features change during train-ing. A broader information-theoretic quantity might incorporate tangent geometry. Fisher information cap-tures local parameter sensitivity: it defines a metric on infinitesimal changes to a parameterized distribu-tion, rather than statistical dependence between two random variables. Whether such local geometry can support a global notion of information content remains open. Further questions concern these controls under reparameterization-invariant attacks such as natural gradient, during feature learning, or in the presence of global constraints such as barriers or mode connectiv-ity (Rosati et al., 2026b; Siddiqui et al., 2025; Cheng and Amiri, 2025). These training-time results leave inference-time information bounds intact. 

# 7 CONCLUSION 

Mutual information at release alone cannot universally certify slow fine-tuning recovery. Exact exclusion of an independently sampled dataset and exact label– representation independence both admit a one-step re-covery example, while function-preserving symmetries and training order show how recovery can vary at fixed information. Information removal can still be useful, but a tamper-resistance guarantee must also constrain the dynamics of the attack it is intended to resist.

B.4 Replication details for the BERT unlearning experiment 

The scrub trains target-class hidden representations toward independently sampled retain-feature vectors while optimizing retain-class cross-entropy. Target accuracy falls to 53 .4±3.6% , with retain accuracy 96 .3±1.2% . Lay-erwise logistic-decoder estimates decrease from 0.433 /0.449 /0.457 to 0.178 /0.150 /0.115 nats; these are estimates of predictive V-information (Xu et al., 2020) for the specified decoder class, not Shannon MI. The scrubbed model recovers in 252 ± 67 updates across three seeds, compared with > 1,000 for random initial-ization. A pretrained control with no target adaptation or scrub reaches the threshold in 75 ± 5 updates. This Removing Information Content Does Not Certify Tamper Resistance
